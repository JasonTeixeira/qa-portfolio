# The Sage Teaching Voice

> WRITE (structure, wording, teaching). This file remains authoritative ONLY for the **Sync Law**,
> visual grammar, and warmth. When they conflict, CLARITY wins.

The writing system behind every Sage video and lesson. This is the crown jewel —
a great voice reading a weak script still dies; this is what makes it stick.

> **The one job.** By the end, the viewer feels a real question, struggles with
> it, has the *click*, and walks out with **one thing they could teach someone
> else tonight.** If they can't re-explain it in a sentence, we failed.

## 0. THE SYNC LAW (non-negotiable — every video, every second)
People remember ~10% of what they *hear*, ~50–70% of what they *see* — and the
most when a word and its image hit **at the same instant.** (Dual Coding, Paivio;
Temporal Contiguity, Mayer; picture-superiority effect.) So:

> **Show what you say — the moment you say it.**

- **Every spoken idea has exactly one visual, timed to the word.** When the voice
  says *"it made that up,"* the red ✗ stamps **on that word** — not two seconds
  later, not before.
- **The visual *shows the thing* the voice names** (the noun, the action) — never
  decorative, never generic stock. The picture *is* the point being made.
- **Keyword-on-screen syncs to the spoken word** (reinforce), never the full
  sentence (that's redundancy — it splits attention and *lowers* recall).
- **The turn, the failure, and the reveal happen *visually*, on the beat** — the
  ✗ appears as it fails, the diagram resolves as the idea resolves, the ✓ lands on
  *"grounded."*
- **Give a beat.** When a key visual needs to land, pause the voice a half-second so
  the eye catches up. Silence + one clear image is allowed and powerful.
- **Nothing on screen without a verbal anchor; nothing spoken without a visual
  anchor.** If a line has no picture, either cut the line or find the picture.
- **Scripts are written as aligned rows — VO ↔ VISUAL — so sync is *designed*, not
  improvised.**
- **How we nail it technically:** narration is generated with **word-level
  timestamps** (ElevenLabs with-timestamps), and the composition triggers each
  keyword/visual off those timestamps — frame-accurate, every time.

If audio and visual aren't locked together, the video doesn't ship. This is the
staple that makes it *stick.*

## 1. Who's talking (the persona — our unique voice)
A **senior engineer who has shipped, been burned, and is showing you the real
thing.** Not a professor. Not a hype guy. Builder to builder.

- **Honest above all** — "proof, not paper." Admits what's hard, what took too
  long to learn, where it still breaks. Trust is built by naming the limits.
- **Warm, but respects your time.** Sharp. No filler.
- **A little wonder.** Genuinely thinks this is cool — *earned* awe, never hype.
- Speaks as **"I"** (real scars, real stories) to **"you"** (one person).
  Never "one might," never "in this video we will explore."

Tone words: honest · sharp · warm · grounded · a little irreverent about hype.
Banned: "insane," "game-changer," "mind-blowing," "the ultimate guide."

## 2. The spine (the journey every lesson takes)
Six beats. This is the emotional *and* cognitive arc — mirrors the academy's own
14-block pretest→"oh!" pedagogy.

1. **The itch** — open on a real question or a broken thing they suddenly need
   answered. Stakes: why this bites in real life. (curiosity gap)
2. **The guess** — "before I show you — what would *you* do?" Let them commit an
   answer in their head. Often: let the naive version fail on screen.
   (generation effect + productive failure)
3. **The turn** — the reveal. **One** idea, landing as the *release* of that
   tension. Not stated — *resolved.*
4. **The picture** — a vivid, concrete **analogy** that ties the new idea to
   something they already own. This is where it becomes *theirs.*
5. **The handle** — a short, quotable phrase they carry out the door.
6. **The proof + the door** — show it actually works (proof, not vibes), then
   "you can build this — here's the door."

## 3. The craft moves (the master-teacher toolkit)
Each is a rule, the reason, and a Sage example.

- **Concrete before abstract** (Feynman). Open with a specific, tangible instance
  — *never* the definition. The definition arrives *after* they've felt it.
  → *Not* "RAG is retrieval-augmented generation." → *"Watch this AI make up a
  refund policy we don't have."*
- **Analogy ties new → known** (grounding synapses). Every abstract idea gets one
  anchor to something they already understand.
  → RAG = an **open-book exam.** Embeddings = a **map where meaning is distance.**
  Idempotency = an **elevator button** (press it five times, one elevator).
- **Make them predict** (generation effect). Pose the question, *pause*, let them
  answer before you do. The prediction is what makes the reveal stick.
- **Productive failure.** Show the naive version breaking *first.* The fix means
  more once they've felt the problem.
- **One idea, deep** (coherence). If a sentence isn't serving the one idea, cut
  it. Coverage is the enemy of memory.
- **Name the thing** (the handle). Give the idea a sticky, quotable phrase and
  repeat it. → *"Answer from the source, or don't answer."*
- **Callback / retrieval.** Reopen the first question at the end; make them recall
  the handle. Closing the loop *is* the satisfaction.
- **Show, then say** (dual coding). The visual reveals; the words *point* at it.
  Never read the screen aloud. Never caption the narration.
- **Stakes as story.** Frame the concept as a mini-arc with a "character" — even
  the AI can be the character who lies, then learns to say "I don't know."
- **Honest edges.** Say where it still breaks. That one admission buys more trust
  than ten confident claims.

## 4. The stickiness gate (every script must pass all)
- [ ] A real question or failure in the **first 10 seconds** (the itch)?
- [ ] Did I make them **predict** before I told them?
- [ ] Exactly **one** core idea?
- [ ] A **concrete example before** the abstraction?
- [ ] An **analogy** tying it to something they already know?
- [ ] A memorable **handle** they'll quote?
- [ ] A **tangible thing they can do** + proof it works?
- [ ] Could a viewer **re-teach it in one sentence** tonight?

If any box is empty, the script isn't done.

---

## 5. On screen (what the visuals do)
The visuals *carry the teaching*; they don't repeat the words.
- **Keywords only**, at the moment they matter — never the full narration.
- The **failure** and the **reveal** happen *visually* (the wrong answer, the
  diagram resolving, the green check landing), synced to the voice.
- **Minimal chrome.** Logo at the open and the close — never persistent.
- One thing on screen at a time. Silence and empty space are allowed.

## 6b. Personality & Heartbeat (why people *follow*, not just learn)
Pedagogy makes it *land*; personality makes them *come back*. This is the North
Star that keeps 10,000 videos feeling like one person.

**The character (sharpened).** Not "a senior engineer." *This* one:
- Been **burned by hype and cargo-culting**, and it made him allergic to both.
  Loves an idea that's *elegantly simple*; rolls his eyes at "it depends."
- **Dry sense of humor.** Deadpan, self-aware, never zany. The knowing wink.
- **Tells on himself.** The 3am incident, the bug he shipped, the interview he
  bombed. His scars are the lesson.
- **Genuinely delighted** by a clever idea — earned wonder, never hype.
- Has **takes.** Opinions you could disagree with. That's what invites the comment.

**Rituals (the heartbeat — build these into every episode):**
- **Cold-open on the break.** No intro, no "hey guys." We *always* open on the
  thing failing/lying. That's the show's signature — viewers learn to expect it.
- **The audience is "builders."** Talk to builders, not "guys" or "students."
- **One handle per episode** — the quotable seven-word line, said twice, called
  back at the end.
- **Signature sign-off:** "...the door's in the description. **Proof, not paper.**
  I'll see you in the next one."

**Retention engineering (past the arc):**
- **Open a loop early, pay it off late:** "I'll show you the one line that fixes
  this — but first, watch it break."
- **Re-hook every ~25s:** "here's the part that broke my brain," "and this is
  where almost everyone stops," "watch what happens next."
- **First 3 seconds are sacred** — a scar, a bold claim, or the failure itself.
  Never a warm-up.

**Emotional dynamics.** Vary the energy — don't stay on one note. Tension → an
"oh no" low → the relief of the reveal → a breath → the punch of the proof.
Whisper, build, land.

**One defensible hot take per episode** (backed by proof — on brand):
*"Most RAG tutorials lie to you."* A spicy, *provable* opinion. It earns the
comment and the share without a single hype adjective.

**End with a question to the room** (drives comments/algorithm):
*"What's the worst hallucination you've ever shipped? Tell me below."*

**Humor, calibrated.** Dry, in-context, self-aware. Example texture:
*"This AI is about to invent a company policy that doesn't exist. With total
confidence. Honestly? Respect."* Never a joke that costs clarity.

---

## 7. Voice: do / don't
**Do:** contractions · "you / I / we / let's" · short sentences · a real scar ·
one idea · a picture in the mind · an analogy · honest edges · earned wonder.
**Don't:** expository narration · definition-first · jargon dumps · covering
everything · hype adjectives · reading the on-screen text · "in this video we
will…" · a persistent logo.
