Lesson 07 · ~30 min · ends in a passing check

Map the blast radius before you touch anything.

01 · Sprint contract
The outcome
Bound any change before shipping it — in writing, in minutes.
The proof
A blast-radius map with every omission defended.
Don't claim
"Nothing else is affected." You may only claim what the map shows.

Friday, 4:52pm. A one-line config change is "totally safe," and the cache layer disagrees. Three services go quietly stale. Nobody knows for two hours — because nobody drew the radius first.

04 · Pretest — guess before you know
What bounds a change's blast radius?
The size of the diff
Everything that reads what you touched
The number of services deployed
05 · The mental model

A change you can't bound is a change you can't defend. Draw who reads what you're touching — then the diff is a decision, not a gamble.

09 · The pro insight

Staff engineers don't map faster — they map less. Ownership boundaries (SWEBOK §7) tell you where the radius legally stops: at a contract someone else defends.

10 · One sharp check
The CDN was left off the map. When is that defensible?
Never — everything goes on the map
When the cache key is unchanged and that's stated on the map
When the CDN team approved the deploy
11 · Prove it — no vibes

✓ Every out-of-scope node carries a written defense
✓ The lab check passes: radius(config) catches the stale-TTL reader
